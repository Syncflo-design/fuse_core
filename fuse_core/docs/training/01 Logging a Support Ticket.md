# Logging a Support Ticket

*Asking for help, and what happens to the request — Fuse user guide*

## Before you start

### What the ticket is for

Something is not working, or you do not know how to do something, or you can see a better
way. All of it goes through the same button, and it lands with whoever supports your site.

The ticket is **recorded on your own site first, then emailed**. That order matters: if the
email cannot go out for any reason, your request still exists and says so on screen, rather
than disappearing while you wait for a reply that was never coming.

### What you do not have to explain

Fuse attaches all of this by itself:

- Which site you are on, and the address of it
- Who you are, and which company you were in
- The versions of Fuse, ERPNext and Frappe you are running
- **The screen you were on** before you clicked the tile

So there is no need to describe your setup, and no need to guess at a version number. Say
what happened; the rest is already there.

## Raising one

1. On Fuse Home, click **Log a support ticket**.
2. **Subject** — one line. What you would say if you were phoning.
3. **What kind of thing is it** — see the table below. It decides who picks it up and how
   fast, so it is worth a moment.
4. **How urgent** — *Whenever you can*, *Soon*, or *Blocking work right now*. Use the last
   one when people genuinely cannot get on, and it will keep meaning something.
5. **What happened** — what you did, and what the screen did. If there was an error
   message, the exact words help far more than a description of them.
6. **What you expected** — only where it is not obvious. It is often the line that turns a
   bug report into a fix.
7. **Screenshot or file** — a picture of the screen as it was, more often than not.
8. **Save**.

That is the whole thing. Saving sends it.

## Choosing the right kind

| Kind | Use it when |
|---|---|
| Something is broken | It errors, or a screen will not do what it is supposed to |
| Something looks wrong | It worked, but the numbers or the data are not right |
| I need training | You or your team need showing how, rather than a fix |
| We need it to work differently | The system does what it was built to do, and that is not what you need |
| Nice to have | An idea. Nothing is waiting on it |
| New user or access | Somebody needs an account, a password reset, or different permissions |
| Something with Intacct | The connection, or a posting that did not reach Intacct |

**Broken** and **looks wrong** are separated on purpose. A screen that throws an error is
loud and obvious. A quantity that quietly disagrees with Intacct is the more serious of the
two and gets buried if both go in the same pile.

## What happens next

### Faults go straight away

Anything reported as broken, looking wrong, or an Intacct problem is emailed the moment you
save it. Nobody has to approve a fault.

The reply comes back to **your** email address, not to a general mailbox, so you can just
answer it.

### Requests may wait for approval

If your site is set up that way, four kinds wait for somebody on your side to sign them
off first: training, a change to how something works, a nice-to-have, and a new user or
access request.

These are the ones that commit somebody's time or money, which is why your organisation may
want a say before they are raised.

Your ticket shows **Waiting for approval** and nothing has been sent yet. Whoever holds the
approver role sees it in their to-do list and either:

- **Approves it** — it is sent, and you can see when and to whom; or
- **Declines it** — it is not sent, and the reason is written on the ticket. You are told,
  and the reason is there to read.

If nobody at your site holds the approver role, nothing waits — everything is sent. A
request that sits behind an approval nobody can give is a request that vanishes, so it is
deliberately not possible.

## Finding your tickets again

Everything you have raised is in the Fuse Support Ticket list, with its status:

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| Waiting for approval | With your approver. Not sent yet |
| Sent | It is with support. Look for a reply in your email |
| Declined | Your approver said no. The reason is on the ticket |
| Could not send | The site could not send the email. Nothing is lost — see below |

## Common questions

### It says "Could not send"

Your site cannot send email at the moment, usually because no outgoing mail account has
been set up on it. Your request is safe — it is saved here in full.

Tell your administrator, and once mail is working use the **Send to support** button on the
ticket. Nothing has to be retyped.

### Who approves my requests?

Whoever your organisation gave the **Fuse Support Approver** role to — usually one or two
people. If you do not know who, your administrator does.

### My request was declined and I still need it

Talk to whoever declined it; their reason is on the ticket. If the situation has changed,
raise it again and say what changed. A declined ticket is a decision at a point in time,
not a permanent no.

### Can I raise a ticket for someone else?

Yes, but the reply goes to whoever raised it. If it is really their problem, it is usually
faster for them to raise it themselves — they can answer the questions that come back.

### Where does it actually go?

To the support address set on your site. For a site supported by Syncflo that is
support@syncflo.co.za; if you bought Fuse through a Sage partner, it goes to their desk
instead. Either way the ticket itself shows exactly where it was sent.
